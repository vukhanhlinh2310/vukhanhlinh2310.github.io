The network: where anticipatory shipping happens

Dairy products with a 14-day shelf life move from a central warehouse through a distribution centre to a cluster of stores.

Normal shipping:  AS (shipper forecasts and ships ahead) or  Pull (order on request) Rescue 1: lateral transshipment Rescue 2: emergency shipment

From forecast bias to shipping cost

Two pipelines: real data for the forecasting comparison, a simulation for the shipping decisions.

Data
Favorita grocery sales
  • Public Kaggle dataset
  • Dairy family, perishable items
  • Daily store-level sales, 2015–2017
  • Store, cluster and city structure
→
RQ1 · Forecasting
Six methods, compared on bias
  • Naive & moving average
  • Holt-Winters, SARIMA
  • Causal regression (promotions)
  • XGBoost
  • Direction, size and structure of bias
→
RQ2 · Simulation
Anticipatory shipping model
  • AS → LT → ES escalation
  • Allocation, placement, frequency
  • Holding, waste, stockout & shipping costs
  • AS vs Pull per network leg
→
RQ3 · Evaluation
Accuracy vs. cost
  • Is the most accurate method also the cheapest?
  • Which bias components and which echelon drive cost?
→
RQ4 · Optimisation
Best method per leg
  • 6 × 6 grid of method combinations
  • CW → DC and DC → store
  • Lowest total cost at target service
Pipeline A · real data onlyForecasts are trained and tested on real sales, with actual sales kept strictly separate as ground truth to avoid data leakage.
Pipeline B · hybrid simulationDemand patterns and network come from the real data; prices, markdowns and deterioration are set from literature and tested with sensitivity analysis.
